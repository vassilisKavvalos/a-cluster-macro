02 · Affordability

Purchasing-power-adjusted electricity prices

PPS places the same expenditure in a cross-country purchasing-power framework. A country can therefore look moderate in euro terms but materially more expensive once household purchasing power is considered.

How to read PPS: PPS is not a currency quote. It is a comparative unit designed to neutralize differences in national price levels, making cross-country affordability comparisons more meaningful.
03 · Typical household

Band DC: 2,500–4,999 kWh per year

Band DC is used here as the core household reference band. It offers a cleaner comparison than aggregating very low- and very high-consumption households into one headline number.

Greece: the latest Band DC reading is about 0.29 PPS per kWh. Its relative position should be interpreted against the EU benchmark and the wider European distribution rather than as a standalone number.

05 · Greece vs Europe

The post-energy-shock path

The time-series view is designed to separate the Greece–EU signal from the broader European dispersion. The background lines show the cross-country range; Greece and the EU benchmark carry the analytical focus.

What to watch: the key issue is not only whether Greek prices rise or fall, but whether Greece is converging toward or diverging from the European benchmark after the 2022 energy-price shock.
