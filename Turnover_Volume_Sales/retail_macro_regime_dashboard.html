Is real consumption expanding — or losing momentum?

A standardized retail-demand regime framework built from Eurostat monthly sales-volume data. The dashboard combines real retail growth, short-run momentum and discretionary non-food demand into a single A-CLUSTER Retail Cycle Indicator (RCI), then decomposes the signal into food, non-food, fuel and online retail.

In June 2026, EU27 real retail sales were +1.4% above a year earlier, but the standardized RCI stood at +0.02, placing the aggregate in a Neutral regime. Breadth remains relatively supportive: 25 of 27 member states recorded positive YoY real retail growth, while 15 had an RCI above zero. The message is therefore not outright contraction, but modest expansion with uneven underlying momentum.

1 · Headline regime: the A-CLUSTER Retail Cycle Indicator

The RCI is an analytical indicator, not an official Eurostat statistic. It is designed to answer a regime question: is retail demand unusually strong or weak relative to its own history?

Indicator construction

Rt = 0.5 · z(YoY real retail) + 0.3 · z(3M momentum) + 0.2 · z(non-food YoY)

Each component is standardized against the geography's own January 2010–June 2026 history. To prevent exceptional pandemic observations from dominating the scale, the standardization uses 1st/99th percentile winsorization before calculating the mean and standard deviation.

2 · Composition: what is driving the retail regime?

The headline signal is decomposed into essential consumption, discretionary non-food demand, automotive fuel and online/mail-order retail. This helps distinguish broad consumption strength from category-specific movements.

Non-food excluding fuel is given the highest compositional importance because it is the closest available signal in this dataset to discretionary retail demand. Food is more defensive, while fuel can be heavily influenced by mobility and energy-price dynamics. Online retail is shown as a structural channel rather than treated as a separate macro regime.

How to read the dashboard

Headline

Use the RCI to classify the regime. A positive YoY growth rate can coexist with a neutral or negative RCI when growth is weak relative to the country's own historical norm or when momentum is deteriorating.

Composition

Compare food with non-food ex fuel. If non-food is leading, discretionary demand is stronger. If food is positive but non-food weak, the apparent resilience is more defensive than cyclical.

Breadth

Cross-country breadth helps determine whether the retail regime is widespread or concentrated in a small set of member states. A strong EU aggregate with weak breadth would be a less convincing expansion signal.
